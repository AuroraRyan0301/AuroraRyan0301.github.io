Estimation in Differentiable Volume Rendering

TL;DR

The extinction gradient splits into a scattering term (evaluated at a path vertex) and a transmittance term (integrated along the segment). The two share the same field, enter the gradient with opposite signs, and are therefore naturally negatively correlated when sampled at the same location — yet every existing estimator samples them at uncorrelated positions, foreclosing that correlation.

We propose sample matching: evaluate both at a single shared probe by reformulating the per-segment integrand as a joint distribution on a triangular support. The resulting unbiased Monte Carlo estimator reduces gradient variance by up to 80% over DRT, with faster convergence and cleaner reconstructions of heterogeneous participating media.

Method at a Glance

For each ray segment we rewrite the variance source of the extinction gradient as an integral on a 2D triangular domain — one axis is the segment depth, the other is the probe depth. Standard DRT picks two independent samples from this domain, losing the cancellation between the scattering and transmittance terms.

Sample matching draws a single sample from a joint density on the same triangle, so the two terms are evaluated at the same shared probe. The estimator remains unbiased, but the inherited negative correlation collapses much of the variance. The construction generalises across participating-media path integrators and composes with existing gradient-tracing strategies.
