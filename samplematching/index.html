TL;DR

The extinction gradient splits into a scattering term (evaluated at a path vertex) and a transmittance term (integrated along the segment). The two share the same field, enter the gradient with opposite signs, and are therefore naturally negatively correlated when sampled at the same location — yet every existing estimator samples them at uncorrelated positions, foreclosing that correlation.

We propose sample matching: evaluate both at a single shared probe by reformulating the per-segment integrand as a joint distribution on a triangular support. The resulting unbiased Monte Carlo estimator reduces gradient variance by up to 80% over DRT, with faster convergence and cleaner reconstructions of heterogeneous participating media.

Toy: scattering & transmittance compete

Drag the probes along the ray segment. Each contributes a piece of the per-segment extinction gradient — scattering with a positive sign, transmittance with a negative one. When the two probes are locked (sample matching), they read the same field and the terms cancel; when they wander independently (DRT), the cancellation is lost and only shows up as variance over many samples.
